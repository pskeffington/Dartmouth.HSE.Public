# Genomics companion

[Paul's Notes](../../README.md) · [Resources](../README.md)

Start with the people and study design behind a dataset, then learn what a gene measurement can support. These public teaching guides explain breast cancer research concepts and project-level statistics; they contain no patient records or private analysis results.

[60-Gene Breast Cancer Research Companion](BREAST_CANCER_60_GENE_COMPANION.md) — expands the retained 21-gene foundation with 39 public teaching selections, complete cards, learning routes and evidence boundaries.

## Read in order

1. [Before the Heatmap: The People Behind Breast Cancer Data](BREAST_CANCER_TCGA_BRCA_NARRATIVE.md) — worldwide burden, the TCGA-BRCA resource, case/sample/file distinctions, paired tissue comparisons, molecular subtypes and responsible interpretation.
2. [Common Breast Cancer Genes: A Reader's Descriptor Guide](COMMON_BREAST_CANCER_GENE_DESCRIPTORS.md) — 21 common genes, biological roles, measurement caveats and gene-specific annotation records.
3. [Visualization gallery](../VISUALIZATION_GALLERY.md) — independently simulated examples for learning how to read scientific figures.

## Use the right denominator

The narrative distinguishes 2024 female incidence and mortality from five-year prevalence for both sexes. Its TCGA-BRCA case count is an access-date snapshot, not a sample count, matched-pair count or population survey. Gene-expression patterns require defined specimens, units, annotation and models; they do not establish clinical biomarker status or a treatment decision.

## Reference scope

WHO and IARC provide population context; NCI describes public research resources and clinical biomarker concepts; HGNC and NCBI provide gene annotation. Links identify these external sources. The guides are educational explanations, not a replication study, a proprietary assay or clinical decision software.
