## B. What this gene normally does

FOXC1 regulates transcription in developmental and cell-identity programs. Its effect depends on the genes accessible in a particular cellular state. This molecular-function explanation is independently written and checked against the linked NCBI Gene record; it is separate from the cancer-study interpretation.

## C. Why breast cancer researchers study it

Understand a basal-associated research marker without turning it into a single-gene subtype classifier.

FOXC1 was investigated as a basal-like-associated regulator and potential prognostic marker. This finding is confined to the study design and model described in section H.

## D. Molecular subtype context

The reviewed FOXC1 study reports a basal-like association. It does not establish a rule separating luminal A, luminal B and HER2-enriched tumors from one FOXC1 value. PAM50 uses a defined multi-gene expression method; clinical ER, PR and HER2 categories use separate assays. [Supervised risk predictor of breast cancer based on intrinsic subtypes. (2009)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204204/).

## E. What the measurement means

Protein expression and target-gene experiments add context to RNA abundance.

DNA mutation changes sequence; copy number changes genomic dosage; RNA measures transcripts; total protein measures abundance; phosphorylation or another activity assay addresses regulation. A clinical laboratory result also depends on specimen, assay and validated criteria. None of these is a substitute for the others. See the [measurement guide](../GENE_EXPRESSION_INTERPRETATION.md).

## G. What this gene cannot tell us

FOXC1 alone cannot establish Basal-like or triple-negative disease. Basal-like classification and clinical triple-negative status overlap incompletely; an association is not a standalone classifier.
