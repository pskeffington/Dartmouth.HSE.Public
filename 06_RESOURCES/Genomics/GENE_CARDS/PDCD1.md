## B. What this gene normally does

PDCD1 encodes PD-1, a receptor that can restrain immune-cell signaling. Its effects depend on ligands, cell state and location. This molecular-function explanation is independently written and checked against the linked NCBI Gene record; it is separate from the cancer-study interpretation.

## C. Why breast cancer researchers study it

Distinguish a receptor on responding immune cells from the PD-L1 ligand encoded by CD274.

The investigators modeled relationships between imaging features, PDCD1 expression and outcomes. This finding is confined to the study design and model described in section H.

## D. Molecular subtype context

The reviewed evidence does not establish a gene-specific discriminator across luminal A, luminal B, HER2-enriched and basal-like disease. The study context below should not be promoted to a four-subtype claim. PAM50 uses a defined multi-gene expression method; clinical ER, PR and HER2 categories use separate assays. [Supervised risk predictor of breast cancer based on intrinsic subtypes. (2009)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204204/).

## E. What the measurement means

Cell-specific receptor staining and immune-function measurements complement RNA.

DNA mutation changes sequence; copy number changes genomic dosage; RNA measures transcripts; total protein measures abundance; phosphorylation or another activity assay addresses regulation. A clinical laboratory result also depends on specimen, assay and validated criteria. None of these is a substitute for the others. See the [measurement guide](../GENE_EXPRESSION_INTERPRETATION.md).

## G. What this gene cannot tell us

PDCD1 RNA cannot prove immune exhaustion or identify treatment eligibility. Prediction of expression is not validation of checkpoint-inhibitor benefit or a new clinical assay.
