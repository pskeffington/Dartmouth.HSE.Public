## B. What this gene normally does

TP53 encodes p53, which helps coordinate responses to cellular stress, including arrest, repair and cell death. The outcome depends on stress and cellular context. This molecular-function explanation is independently written and checked against the linked NCBI Gene record; it is separate from the cancer-study interpretation.

## C. Why breast cancer researchers study it

Distinguish a tumor's DNA variant from RNA abundance and a functional stress response.

TP53 mutations were examined for breast-cancer-specific survival associations, including ER-positive disease. This finding is confined to the study design and model described in section H.

## D. Molecular subtype context

The reviewed evidence does not establish a gene-specific discriminator across luminal A, luminal B, HER2-enriched and basal-like disease. The study context below should not be promoted to a four-subtype claim. PAM50 uses a defined multi-gene expression method; clinical ER, PR and HER2 categories use separate assays. [Supervised risk predictor of breast cancer based on intrinsic subtypes. (2009)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204204/).

## E. What the measurement means

Sequencing, protein staining and target-gene response are complementary assays.

DNA mutation changes sequence; copy number changes genomic dosage; RNA measures transcripts; total protein measures abundance; phosphorylation or another activity assay addresses regulation. A clinical laboratory result also depends on specimen, assay and validated criteria. None of these is a substitute for the others. See the [measurement guide](../GENE_EXPRESSION_INTERPRETATION.md).

## G. What this gene cannot tell us

TP53 RNA cannot establish mutation status or whether p53 is functioning normally. Historical ascertainment and treatment context limit transport; p53 RNA does not specify mutation or functional status.

## I. Student learning takeaway

1. **Function:** TP53 encodes p53, which helps coordinate responses to cellular stress, including arrest, repair and cell death.
2. **Measurement:** Sequencing, protein staining and target-gene response are complementary assays.
3. **Inference:** TP53 RNA cannot establish mutation status or whether p53 is functioning normally.

**Scientific question:** Why might an altered p53 protein be abundant while its normal response is impaired?

**Common mistake:** Treating the study's association or experimental endpoint as a validated individual diagnosis, subtype or treatment prediction.
